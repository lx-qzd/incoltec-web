With the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) banning Titanium Dioxide (E171) and increasing scrutiny on artificial dyes like Red 40 (Allura Red) and Yellow 5 (Tartrazine), food manufacturers are facing unprecedented challenges in product formulation.

## The Push for Clean Labels

Consumers today are reading ingredient lists more carefully than ever. A "clean label" is no longer a premium niche—it's an industry standard. Natural colors derived from fruits, vegetables, and edible plants are leading the charge.

### Key Alternatives

1. **Beetroot Red (E162):** An excellent replacement for synthetic reds, particularly stable in acidic environments like yogurts and fruit preparations.
2. **Beta-Carotene (E160a):** The gold standard for achieving vibrant yellow and orange hues in beverages and dairy, naturally sourced from carrots and algae.
3. **Modified Starch Blends:** For opacity and whitening (the void left by E171), new proprietary blends like INCOLTEC's Linicol CF Blanco are providing the necessary cloudiness and bright white base required in confectionery.

## Stability Challenges

The main hurdle with natural colors is their sensitivity to pH, light, and heat. However, advanced microencapsulation technologies have dramatically improved shelf life and color retention during high-heat processes like baking and UHT pasteurization.

INCOLTEC continues to invest in R&D to ensure that natural colors not only match the vibrancy of their synthetic counterparts but also exceed them in safety and consumer trust.
